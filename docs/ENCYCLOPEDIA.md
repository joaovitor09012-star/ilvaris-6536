# ILVARIS — THE READER'S COPY
### An encyclopedia of the known world, as kept in the Vellum Athenaeum's open stacks

> *"The world is a text. Most people are words in it. A few of us learn to hold the pen.*
> *No one has ever asked who is reading."*
> — opening line of the Vellum Athenaeum's first-year primer

---

## 1. THE BOOK THAT IS THE WORLD

**Ilvaris** is a world that is also a book. Not a metaphor the scholars enjoy — a fact they
have measured. The land has strata like pages, history has drafts, and reality has margins
where the writing went wrong.

Nine hundred years ago the **Ledger** was discovered: the substrate under existence, the
thing that remembers what happened and decides what is true. It is not a god. It does not
want. It records, it balances, and when a page is unbalanced it **writes back**.

Every lecture in the Athenaeum begins from four plain facts:

- **Magic is inscription.** Reality is text; sorcery is writing over it. Everyone who
  practises it is, in the end, an author of small things.
- **Fate is a contract.** The Ledger assigns roles — Commissions — to people whose lives
  it needs for the story it is balancing. Most people never learn they were cast.
- **Erasure is real.** A person who fails the Ledger badly enough is not killed. They are
  **revised**. The world is edited so they were never there. Their mother's memory closes
  over them like water. This is worse than death, and it is the reason the academy's
  students are so serious about attendance.
- **Power costs story.** Workings beyond a certain scale create **Debt**, and Debt is
  settled in events, not coin. The more the world must bend for you, the more it needs
  something to happen to you to make it true.

The **Vellum Athenaeum of Ilvaris** sits on the Fourth Stratum, in a valley the locals
call the Hollow. It exists to train Inscribers — people who can hold the pen without
tearing the page. Its students duel in the courtyards for ink and attention. Its faculty
trade in favours and secrets. And under the floor of its oldest library there is a door
that the Archivist has never once explained.

## 2. WHAT PEOPLE CALL IT

Everyone in Ilvaris lives under the Ledger. Almost no one agrees on what it is.

| Who | What they call it | What they believe |
|---|---|---|
| Peasants | **the Pattern** | Fate. You don't argue with it. |
| The Bound Crown | **the Charter** | The legal basis of the monarchy. It is also literally true, which is worse. |
| The Mercery | **the Market** | The Ledger is supply and demand with better bookkeeping. |
| The Choir of Ash | **the Author** | A loving god who writes us. They are insufferable about it. |
| The Unwritten | **the Cage** | The text should be burned. They are terrorists with a point. |
| The Vellum | **the Curriculum** | The Ledger is a discipline. Teachable, gradeable, and due on Thursday. |
| Students | **the Luck** | A superstitious, exhausted shorthand for causality they cannot see. |

The Ledger's effects are felt rather than seen: coincidence, obligation, the sense that
something is owed. Which of these schools is right is the oldest argument in Ilvaris, and
the Athenaeum has never once settled it.

## 3. THE NINE STRATA

Ilvaris is layered. Each Stratum is a **draft** of the world — an earlier attempt at the
same story, kept because deleting it would unbalance the whole. You descend by going
*down through* the text, and the deeper you go, the less finished things are.

| # | Stratum | State | What lives there |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | **The Vellum Sky** | ink-wet, unfinished | Weather that hasn't been decided yet |
| II | **The Margins** | annotated | Marginalia in their wild state |
| III | **The Fen of First Draft** | overwritten | Things with too many versions of themselves |
| IV | **The Hollow** | *current, stable* | The Athenaeum and the capital city **Carrow** |
| V | **The Long Footnote** | dense, pedantic | The Registry's archive-vaults; the true records |
| VI | **The Errata** | corrected, scarred | The Culled Courts |
| VII | **The Palimpsest** | scraped and rewritten | Ruins of an older draft — and, some say, its people |
| VIII | **The Binding Floor** | pressure | Where the Ledger settles its heaviest debts |
| IX | **The Blank** | unreadable | No one who has charted it has come back to say |

Descent requires a **Binding** — not a beast exactly, but a role the world needs played
to its end. Every Stratum has one open Binding at any time. Clearing it advances the
Cycle. Failing it is how people get revised.

A Binding's reward is a **Relic** — an object that is also a rule — plus 500 crowns split
among the Circle that claimed it. Relics cannot be bought. This is why Circles fight over
Bindings, and why the politics of the academy and the dangers of the deep are, in the end,
the same story.

## 4. INSCRIPTION — HOW MAGIC WORKS

Hard rules. Every working in this world obeys them.

### Ink
The fuel. Measured in **drams**. A person's reserve is personal and finite.

Ink returns three ways:
1. **Rest and food** — slow, reliable.
2. **Witness** — *being seen doing something meaningful returns ink.* A duel before a crowd
   refills an Inscriber in minutes. A performance does the same. This single fact explains
   the academy's culture: it is full of show-offs because showing off is literally
   nutritious.
3. **Transfer** — taking ink from another. Consensual between lovers, Circles and sworn
   pairs. Otherwise it is the crime of **Drawing**, and it is the worst thing you can do to
   someone short of revising them.

### The Four Grammars (how you write)
| Grammar | Function | Cost | Legality |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Declarative** | Direct imposition. Fire, walls, force. | Heavy ink, heavy Recoil | Open |
| **Interrogative** | Questions. Divination, unraveling, truth-finding. | Light ink, subtle | Open, but nobody trusts a questioner |
| **Imperative** | Commands. Binding, oaths, compulsion of will. | Medium ink, *moral* Recoil | Legal to the third degree; above that, capital |
| **Subjunctive** | The what-if. Illusion, possibility, temporary reality. | Brutal ink, exquisite control | Prestigious. Hardest. Taught last. |

**Subjunctive's deep rule:** an illusion believed by enough witnesses *stops being an
illusion*. This is how Relics are made, how gods have been made, and — the heretics say —
how the Ledger itself was made. First-years are told it once and asked never to test it.

### The Five Threads of Writing (what you write on)
1. **Flesh** — bodies, healing, monsters, growth
2. **Matter** — stone, metal, weather, mechanism
3. **Mind** — perception, memory, dream, fear
4. **Bond** — oaths, contracts, the Mark, Circles
5. **Void** — erasure and the Culled. Forbidden. Taught in one lecture, in the last year, with the door locked.

Each student declares a **primary Thread** at the end of first term and may never fully
change it.

### Recoil, Collateral, Stain
Every working costs more than ink. The difference must be paid in **Collateral**:

- **Blood** — wounds that don't heal clean
- **Years** — ageing. The old masters look it.
- **Memory** — you forget something, and never know what
- **Sensation** — a colour, a taste, the ability to feel warmth
- **Another's** — the crime. Someone else pays your bill.

Unpaid Recoil accumulates as **Stain**: a visible discolouration in a person's text.
Low Stain is a scar, a mark of experience. High Stain draws **Marginalia** — they eat
loose ends, and a Stain-heavy Inscriber is a walking loose end.

### Debt
Workings above a certain scale create **Ledger Debt**. Debt cannot be paid in money. It is
paid in *events*: something must happen that makes the imbalance true, and the Ledger
will see that it does.

> Great workings call great consequences.
> Nothing is free, and nothing is wasted.
> — carved over the door of the Chair of Bond

## 5. THE LAWS THAT DO NOT BEND

What the Athenaeum teaches as settled. No scholar has ever disproved one, and several have
been revised for trying.

1. The Ledger exists and cannot be destroyed, only edited.
2. Erasure is retroactive and permanent to everyone except the erased.
3. No working creates something from nothing. Magic rewrites, it never invents.
4. Death is death. There is no resurrection — only revision, which is not the same person.
5. The Nine Strata are drafts of one story, not nine worlds.
6. What was written, happened. Only Void grammar can touch the past, and it charges in full.
7. Every great matter leaves a scar on the world, whether or not anyone stepped in.

## 6. THE SIX THREADS

There is a story told quietly in the Athenaeum's dormitories, and the faculty do not tell
it at all.

Six women at the academy carry Commissions that the Ledger has already written to an end.
Their roles are not secret — the titles are on file with the Registry, and anyone may read
them. What is written *after* the titles is another matter. No one admits to having seen
it. Everyone who has tried describes the same thing: the endings are not happy, and they
are coming soon.

| Name | The role she was cast in | What is said of her |
|---|---|---|
| **Sera Vane** | *The Sword That Was Promised* | The finest duelist of her year. She fights as though she has nothing to save it for, and spends this term as though it were her last. |
| **Ilse Marrow** | *The Archivist's Ward* | Raised inside the library; knows its margins by heart. Sometimes she stops mid-sentence, as if someone else were speaking. |
| **Nadia Rooke** | *The Heiress* | The Mercery's only child. Her marriage is being arranged, and she has not been asked. |
| **Tove Ashgrove** | *The Quiet Hand* | An unremarkable student, by every record the Registry keeps. The records are very sure of it. |
| **Wren Sable** | *The Culled* | Few people remember her the same way twice — including, it is said, Wren herself. |
| **Halcyon "Hal" Ferre** | *The Saint of Ash* | The Choir's beloved, who hears the Author and adores it. Kind to everyone. Notices more than she says. |

A Commission is not a sentence, the old texts insist; it is a *role*. A role can be played
out in more than one way. Love, enmity, rescue, refusal — the Ledger is said to count any
ending that is truly an ending. What it will not abide is a thread left to fray.

Whether a stranger can change a fate already written is a question no one at the
Athenaeum has been able to answer. Until this term, no one had a reason to ask.

## 7. THE NEW STUDENT

This term the Athenaeum admitted a student on a scholarship nobody can account for.

- **A native of Ilvaris**, nineteen, newly arrived in the Hollow.
- **A scholarship without a name.** Someone paid the tuition. Nobody will say who.
- **A blank first page.** Every Inscriber's Codex opens on the Commission the Registry has
  filed for them. This one opens on nothing. The Registry cannot file it. The Archivist
  finds this personally insulting.
- **Provisional standing**, 40 shards, one apprentice stylus, no declared Thread, no
  Circle.

That has never happened before. The Registry has opened an inquiry. The student body has
opened a betting book.

## 8. COIN AND INK

### Currency
**shard** (copper) → 100 shards = 1 **mark** (silver) → 100 marks = 1 **crown** (gold)

Ink is money's twin: **raw ink trades at 2 marks a dram**, and it is consumable. A
household's savings and a mage's ammunition are the same asset. This is why the Mercery
is more powerful than the Crown.

### Price board (Fourth Stratum, autumn term)
| Good / service | Price |
|---|---|
| Bowl of barley, bread | 8 shards |
| Tavern bed, shared | 30 shards |
| Student's coat, decent | 3 marks |
| Term tuition (full fee) | 40 marks |
| Apprentice stylus | 12 marks |
| Journeyman stylus | 4 crowns |
| Master stylus | 30 crowns |
| Raw ink, 1 dram | 2 marks |
| A first-year's full working (2–5 drams) | 4–10 marks in ink alone |
| Temple healing, minor | 2 crowns |
| Raid-grade ward, single use | 60 crowns |
| Horse | 8 crowns |
| Blot bounty | 5 shards |
| Annotation bounty | 40 shards |
| Gloss bounty | 3 marks |
| Erratum bounty | 2 crowns |
| Stratum Binding reward | 500 crowns + Relic, split by the claiming Circle |
| **Tithe** (the Crown's cut on registered workings) | 8% of ink spent |

Wages: labourer 20 shards/day · clerk 60 shards/day · Regent 4 marks/day · a working
Inscriber 1–3 crowns/day *when employed*.

### How prices move
Every merchant in Carrow knows these by heart:

- **When a Binding is cleared**, loot floods in: weapons and wards fall by a third, and ink
  spikes as every Circle prepares for the next one.
- **When a trade road is cut**, grain triples in three days. The poor riot in six.
- **When the Mercery corners ink**, there is rationing, a black market at three times the
  price, cancelled practical classes — and a great many students with a grudge.
- **Those who corner a market** tend to find that the world takes an interest in them.
- **Hoarders** raise local prices and lose standing. Circles notice misers.

The Mercery keeps its price-books open to anyone who asks. They are, irritatingly, honest.

## 9. BESTIARY

### Marginalia — creatures born from drafting errors
Not demons. **Typos with teeth.** They feed on loose ends: unfinished business, unpaid
debts, high Stain, unanswered questions.

| Tier | Name | Nature | Threat |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Blots** | Vermin. Ink-wet smudges that scuttle. Breed in margins of crowded rooms. | Low. Dangerous in swarm. |
| 2 | **Annotations** | Predatory. Scribbled limbs, one long line for a mouth. Hunt the Stain-marked. | Medium. Ambush predators. |
| 3 | **Glosses** | Intelligent mimics. Wear a copy of a person's mannerisms and a face that is *nearly* right. Ask questions to learn what they're imitating. | High. Social horror. |
| 4 | **Errata** | Elite. Reality-bending. A single Erratum can unwrite a room's exit, a wound, a name. | Extreme. Requires a Circle. |

### The Culled
Things that were revised and did not stop. A Culled is an **absence shaped like a
person** — a hole in the world that walks, remembers, and resents. They cannot be
remembered by anyone who knew them; you forget them seconds after meeting them, which
makes fighting one a nightmare of re-introductions.

### Natural fauna
Ilvaris is a real place too. Bears, wolves, crag-lions, river-eels, war-horses. Most
deaths in the Hollow are ordinary ones.

### Bound things
A Stratum's **Binding** is not a monster. It is a *role made flesh*: the world has decided
something must happen, and has grown a shape to make it happen. The Fourth Binding is
said to be duelist-shaped, and to wait for a duelist.

### What the deep keeps
Expeditions to the lowest Strata bring back the same story in different words: something
down there edits its own edges, and has been doing so for a long time. The Athenaeum's
official position is that it does not exist. The Athenaeum's official position has been
wrong before.

## 11. HOW THE WORLD REMEMBERS

People in Ilvaris know what they have seen, believe what they have been told, and act on
both with the same confidence. A rumour repeated in the refectory can be a fact by the
Thursday market. A lie told well enough can outlive the liar.

Nothing is undone by sleeping on it. What happened, happened; who saw it remembers
it; who heard of it believes some version of it. The Ledger forgets nothing — and neither,
it turns out, does a dormitory.

## 12. SIGNS OF THE SEASON

What the Hollow is talking about as the autumn term begins:

- **The Registry's audit.** The new intake is being filed. One file will not close.
- **The price of ink.** The Mercery's quotes have crept up three weeks running. Nobody
  official has said why.
- **The Fourth Binding.** Older students say it will open before the year is out. The
  duelling circles have started training as if it already has.
- **Quiet nobles.** A handful of Carrow's great houses have begun agreeing with each other.
  Their servants find this more alarming than the arguing.
- **The door under the library.** Still closed. Still unexplained.
